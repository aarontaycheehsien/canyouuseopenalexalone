# Transgenerational inheritance evidence map: frozen OQL POST transfer test

Branch `opus5.5`, 12 November 2026 (Asia/Singapore). Written and committed before any candidate search was executed and before included-study identities were parsed.

## Why this benchmark

The request was a benchmark whose original screening load was at least 10,000 records. Selected: **Walker et al. (2018), *Human and animal evidence of potential transgenerational inheritance of health effects: An evidence map and state-of-the-science evaluation*, Environment International 115:48–69, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2017.12.032.**

- **63,754 references title/abstract screened after duplicate removal** (63,789 from PubMed plus references from other sources: the text says 3, the selection diagram says 4).
- **257 included references** (46 human, 211 animal) after full-text review.
- **A single, fully reported PubMed strategy** (Supplemental Table S1), built from text words only because no MeSH heading exists for the topic. This is the cleanest translation target in this repository so far: no controlled vocabulary, one database, no filters, no language or date limits.
- A different domain from the earlier depression, insomnia and diabetic-eye benchmarks: environmental health and toxicology, with animal and human evidence.
- The authors publish the included list (supplement mmc1, "References Included after full-text review") and the HAWC assessment (https://hawcproject.org/assessment/73/).

Alternatives considered: Noetel et al. (2024), already frozen on `main` but not executed, so left alone; Brouwer et al. (2019) and van Dis et al. (2020) from SYNERGY, both depression/anxiety and multi-database; Kennis et al. (2020), already used. The selection was made on review characteristics, not on any OpenAlex retrieval result.

## Information boundary

Inspected before freezing: the article's methods, PECO statement and selection diagram; Supplemental Table S1; the supplement's preamble and structural counts (headings, paragraph counts, how many paragraphs contain a year); HAWC study-list field names and its record count (255); SYNERGY's dataset metadata and file row counts.

**Incidental exposure, recorded honestly:** a structural `head` of the HAWC JSON printed the first included record's title and authors (an ENU-mutagenesis hypocholesterolaemia mouse study, Aigner et al. 2007). No other included-study titles, identifiers or OpenAlex matches were viewed. SYNERGY labels were not read. This is an AI-assisted procedural holdout, not independent blinding, and cannot exclude exposure through model training.

The frozen queries contain no term derived from any included record; they are a mechanical translation of S1.

## Frozen searches

Both arms are generated by `build_queries.py` from `source_search_strategy_S1.json`, submitted as `POST https://api.openalex.org/` with a JSON body `{"oql": ...}`.

**A — faithful, title-abstract.** Every PubMed `[tiab]` term becomes an exact, quoted OQL string; truncation is kept as an OQL wildcard; Boolean structure `(#1 OR #2 OR #3 OR #4 OR #5)` is preserved, with #3 as `(F-terms) AND (generation* OR offspring OR progeny)`.

**B — identical Boolean string on title-abstract-keywords.** The only difference from A is the added OpenAlex keyword field. B is therefore a superset of A, and the A/B comparison isolates the keyword field. (Earlier tests in this repository changed several things between arms at once.)

Shared settings: publication date `<= 2016-11-30` ("PubMed was searched up to November 2016"); default core corpus; no language, type, species, retraction, topic or benchmark-ID restriction.

Known translation departures, decided in advance:

1. S1's #3 F-generation terms are **untagged** in PubMed, so they searched All Fields with automatic term mapping. OpenAlex has no All Fields equivalent; they are searched in the same field as the rest. This narrows #3.
2. PubMed's phrase index and OpenAlex's analyser may tokenise `F(2)`, `F 2`, hyphenated forms and wildcard phrases differently. This is part of what is being tested.
3. PubMed `[tiab]` also covers author keywords and other-abstract fields; arm A covers title and abstract only. Arm B adds OpenAlex keywords, which are not the same as author keywords.
4. A publication-date ceiling is not the same as a PubMed entry-date snapshot. Records published after 2016-11-30 but indexed earlier (or the reverse) can differ. Date-only failures will be reported separately, not repaired.
5. The 3–4 references identified outside the database search stay in the denominator. A Boolean search is not expected to recover them by design, and that is reported rather than excluded.

Free `/query` validation (no execution) returned `valid: true` for both arms; the only warning is that nested Booleans cannot be written as a classic URL. Responses are in `validation/`.

## Benchmark construction (after the freeze)

The denominator is every entry in the supplement's "References Included after full-text review" list. The stated total is 257. If parsing yields a different number, the discrepancy is reconciled against the article and HAWC and documented entry by entry; entries are never silently dropped or merged. Multiple entries mapping to one OpenAlex work stay visible.

Matching order: DOI or PMID where obtainable (HAWC reference metadata, then single-entity lookups); otherwise title/first author/year search. Fuzzy matches require explicit adjudication with the candidate list preserved. Unmatched or ambiguous entries remain in the denominator as unrecovered. Coverage, recall among matched records and recall against the full denominator are reported separately.

## Outcomes

- **Primary recall threshold:** strictly greater than 95% of the full denominator. For N = 257 that is **at least 245/257**.
- **Workload comparison:** API-reported count versus **63,754** records screened. A lower count is a potential screening volume, not a measured time saving; no new records are screened and precision is not estimated.
- Both are reported for both arms. A and B are not chosen between after the fact.

## Execution and evidence

1. For each arm: one POST returning `meta.count`, the executed OQO (`meta.x_query.oqo`) and the first page (`per_page=100`, `sort=publication_date:asc`).
2. **Membership:** the identical executed OQO with only an AND restriction to the matched benchmark IDs, in batches of at most 100. The offline verifier must confirm that nothing else changed.
3. **Export:** an OpenAlex API key (user's own, stored only in git-ignored `.env.txt`) enables cursor paging. Search requests cost about $0.001 each against a $1 daily free budget, so a complete cursor export via POST is attempted only for arms with **count ≤ 40,000** (≤ 400 pages). For larger arms, only the count, first page and restricted membership are kept, and the summary labels recovery as restricted-membership evidence, not a validated complete export. If an export is attempted, page counts, totals, duplicate IDs and the intersection with matched benchmark IDs are checked against the restricted-membership result.
4. **POST execution checks:** re-submitting the echoed OQO by POST must give the same count; cursor continuation by POST must work beyond page 1 with the key.
5. **Secondary, descriptive:** overlap with the SYNERGY `Walker_2018` record set (48,638 rows with 2023 OpenAlex IDs, derived from Howard et al. 2016's export of an earlier or partial version of the same PubMed screening). It is uploaded as a private collection in the user's OpenAlex account and used only to count how many records each arm shares with that set. Screening labels are not used. Merged or missing 2023 IDs are reported. This is a translation-fidelity indicator, not a recall measure.
6. **Post-hoc diagnostics**, run only after primary results are saved and clearly labelled: clause-level tests on missed records (date, each numbered block, field). No repaired query replaces a frozen arm.

Archive every request body, status, response and UTC timestamp, never request headers or the key. Keep source documents and abstracts in scratch storage; archive only bibliographic and API evidence needed for audit. Provide an offline, standard-library verifier.
